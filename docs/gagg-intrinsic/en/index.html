Spectrum and decomposition

The solid red line is the sum of the components with weight 1; the colored fill shows the same components stacked (smallest contribution in the visible range at the bottom, largest at the top; the table below lists them in order of decreasing contribution). The black stepped line is the RC-103G spectrum (unit 1) measured in a lead shield (22–30.12.2024, live time 668,023.5 s); the energy scale of the file was checked at a single point, the ²¹²Pb 238.6 keV line (the slope was not verified; see “Limitations and caveats”). ¹⁷⁶Lu is included in the sum at its upper-limit activity (4.3 Bq/kg; see “Limitations and caveats”), so the sum is an upper estimate with respect to lutetium. The region below 238 keV is shaded: the resolution function there is not confirmed by lines. The main curve uses the Birks parameter kB = 0.0072 (LANL, GAGG High Yield): kB itself was not fitted, but of the four literature values, the one closest to the measurement was chosen for it; hence the closeness of the main curve to the measurement is not a test of the model. The independent prediction is the light-blue band spanning all 4 literature kB values (maximum of the ¹⁵²Gd+¹⁴⁷Sm sum for kB from 0.0121 to 0.0065); the dashed lines mark the measurement, the model maximum, and the Belli formula (comparison).

Components in the visible range

The rate is the integral over the 1 keV bins of the visible range, in s⁻¹; the counts are the rate × 668,023.5 s. The table is recalculated when the plot is zoomed and when “smeared / unsmeared” is toggled. The frame around a name indicates the decay type; the first line under the name states what produces the peak or continuum (positions in keVee, α light yield at kB 0.0072 (LANL), before smearing, histogram step 1 keV; “centroid” in this table is taken from the peak bin, and the true centroid may differ by up to 0.4 keV); the second line gives the basis of the activity. A β particle mostly loses its energy in the crystal (the range of low-energy β particles is much less than 1 cm — an estimate, not checked separately); the accompanying γ rays are registered in the same pulse if they are absorbed (some escape from the cube), so β decays yield a continuum rather than pure γ peaks.

Limitations and caveats

  • The activities are those of a different crystal. The normalization follows Belli et al., J. Phys. G 53 (2026) 045101, Table 1 (a crystal from Epic-Crystal); the activities of the RC-103G crystal were not measured (except for the upper limit for ¹⁷⁶Lu, which was taken from the RC-103G measurement). In particular, ¹⁷⁶Lu and ¹⁴⁷Sm are probably impurities specific to a given manufacturer and in the RC-103G crystal may differ severalfold or be absent. There are two further departures from Table 1 (besides ¹⁷⁶Lu, ¹⁵²Gd, and ¹⁴⁷Sm; see “Initial composition of the crystal”): ²²⁸Ra is taken equal to ²²⁸Th (11.52 mBq/kg; an assumption of equilibrium), whereas Belli et al. give only an upper limit, ≤ 4 mBq/kg, for ²²⁸Ra — in the model ²²⁸Ra is 2.9 times as high as this limit; ²³⁵U (2 mBq/kg) is the upper limit of Belli et al., not a measured value.
  • ¹⁷⁶Lu is included in the sum at its upper limit. Belli et al. give 11 Bq/kg, but this level is incompatible with the RC-103G measurement: in each of the four 100 keV windows in the 400–800 keV range, lutetium alone would give 2.1–2.5 times more counts than the instrument recorded there in total. If the whole residual “measurement − model” in the 600–700 keV window is attributed to lutetium, the limit is ≤ 0.39 of the Belli level, that is, ≤ 4.3 Bq/kg (4309 mBq/kg). ¹⁷⁶Lu is included in the sum with this activity. The limit is an overestimate: the residual also contains the external background of the shield. In the model, lutetium inside the crystal has no narrow 202/307 keV peaks — the β particle and the absorbed part of the γ cascade fall into one pulse, which produces a broad hump at 150–850 keV.
  • No external background yet. The external background (shielding, room, cosmic rays) is not modeled.
  • The nonproportionality of GAGG is not modeled: the light yield of e⁻/γ is taken to be 1:1 with the energy deposition.
  • The α light yield follows Birks’ law with a literature kB; kB is not fitted. Geant4 provides no kB for GAGG; the values are taken from measurements with accelerator beams: 0.0065 g·MeV⁻¹·cm⁻² (Furuno et al., JINST 16 (2021) P10012) and 0.0072 / 0.0093 / 0.0121 (Espinoza et al., IEEE Trans. Nucl. Sci. 71 (2024) 2102, LANL; GAGG types High Yield / Standard / Fast Decay, Advatech crystals). kB is not fitted to the α particles of the intrinsic background or to the RC-103G measurement. However, the main curve was selected using the measurement: of the four literature values, the one closest to it was taken, kB = 0.0072, so its deviation from the measurement (+17.7 keV) is the smallest of the four; it is neither a blind prediction nor a test of the model. The GAGG type in the RC-103G is unknown, so the independent prediction is the entire band of the four kB values, not a single number. Recoil nuclei are assigned a light yield of 0 (an assumption; not calculated).
  • The scale S and the transfer of kB are unverified assumptions. S is derived from the response to 662 keV γ rays, where the electron light yield is quenched by the same law, whereas in the decays the electrons are not quenched (1:1); the two scales are matched only at the 662 keV point, and the shift of the α line near 350 keV has not been estimated. The authors’ kB values were obtained using stopping powers: Furuno — SRIM, LANL — SRIM and ESTAR/PSTAR; in our calculation we use G4EmCalculator (ComputeElectronicDEDX), without recalculating kB. Birks’ law is extrapolated beyond the measured dE/dx interval (up to 200 MeV·cm²·g⁻¹ in Furuno): for α particles of 2–9 MeV, dE/dx is higher at the end of their range.
  • Resolution — the FWHM function = √(a + bE + cE²), a = 75.402 keV², b = 2.4752 keV, c = 3.9295·10⁻⁴, was fitted to the lines at 238.6–2614.5 keV of a single instrument (RC-103G (unit 2)). Below 238 keV the function is not confirmed; it is an extrapolation.
  • Bare cube without housing: the SiPM, the board, and the housing of the RC-103G are absent from the geometry.
  • The 1 µs pulse pile-up window is an assumption: the signal formation in the RC-103G is not known.
  • How the α peak of the model was obtained. Geant4 calculates the α light yield step by step according to Birks’ law (section “What was calculated and how”, step 6); the Belli formula is not part of the calculation. At kB = 0.0072, the centroid of the ¹⁵²Gd line in the templates is 358.98 keVee (peak bin 358.5), and the peak bin of ¹⁴⁷Sm is 377.5; the maximum of the Gd+Sm sum after smearing is 367.5 keV (midpoints of 1 keV bins; the same value is labeled on the plot, and the label is taken from the data). The activities are 792.8 mBq/kg for Gd (calculated from natural gadolinium) and 762 mBq/kg for Sm (the remainder up to the Belli sum of 1555), i.e., almost 1:1, so the maximum of the sum lies between the lines. A peak at 349.8 keV was observed with the RC-103G (unit 1). It is interpreted as the maximum of an unresolved Gd+Sm mixture, on the grounds that the count rate is close to the Gd+Sm sum of the model; however, the peak width (31.5 keV) is closer to that of a single line (in the formula template, the mixture gives 43.0 keV and Gd alone 31.4 keV; for kB 0.0072, 41.4 and 31.9 keV, our recalculation), and this is an open question. Therefore the comparison of the maximum of the sum with 349.8 keV (+17.7 keV) is tentative; moreover, the histogram step is 1 keV, the statistical uncertainty of the positions was not calculated, and the energy scale of the instrument near 350 keV is uncertain by approximately 5 keV (gain drift of the same instrument between July and December 2024). For the four literature kB values, the maximum of the sum ranges from 239.5 keV (kB 0.0121) to 398.5 keV (kB 0.0065), and is 298.5 keV at 0.0093; no value gives 349.8 keV, and reproducing it would require kB ≈ 0.0077 (an estimate by linear interpolation between 0.0072 and 0.0093, not a run; for details see “What was calculated and how”, step 6).
  • Disagreement with the Belli formula. The Belli formula (Eq. 7) is defined for α energies of 2.1–7.4 MeV; the comparison is made with the formula template — the formula applied to the same decays. In this range, no literature kB agrees with the formula simultaneously for ¹⁵²Gd (2.15 MeV) and for ²¹⁵Po (7.39 MeV): at kB 0.0065, +12.09% and +2.02%; at 0.0072, +3.30% and −5.35%. ²¹⁴Po (7.69 MeV) and ²¹²Po (8.79 MeV) lie outside the range of the formula, so for them this is an extrapolation of the formula: α/γ increases from ¹⁵²Gd to ²¹²Po by a factor of 1.42–1.48 in the model and by a factor of 1.61 according to the extended formula. In Belli et al., the α scale above ~2 MeV on the γ scale is distorted by saturation of the digitizer (footnote 8, caption of Fig. 6), but for ²¹²Po (8.79 MeV) they reconstructed the signal shape and obtained a peak at ≈ 2.25 MeV on the γ scale, “in agreement with the α/γ ratio (see equation (7))” (caption of Fig. 10): that is, there is a measurement at this point, and it is closer to the formula (2293.5 keVee) than to the literature kB values 0.0072–0.0121 (−8 … −38% from the formula); kB 0.0065 (2264.5 keVee, −1.3%) is closer still to the measurement, and the accuracy of reading the peak from the figure has not been estimated; the disagreement here is not merely an extrapolation. The maximum of the Gd+Sm sum by the Belli formula is 356.5 keV (the dashed line “Belli formula (comparison)” on the plot; the formula was derived on a different crystal). The table of lines is in the section “What was calculated and how”, step 6.
  • Limit on the α step and the recoil energy. The α step in the crystal is limited to 0.05 µm; because of this, the templates are not bitwise identical to the earlier ones (the stream of random numbers shifts); statistical equivalence to the earlier templates was not verified. The recoil energy in the “raw” column of the template files is overestimated (T2: 94.7 keV per decay for a kinetic energy of 62.9 keV); the cause has not been established — an open question. By construction, the effect on the α light yield is expected to be zero (the light yield of recoil nuclei is taken to be 0 — an assumption), but in the check of the earlier fit variants, the distribution of the light yield of T2 did not scale strictly with S — a shift of 0.3–0.5 keV of undetermined origin.
  • The difference between the measurement and the model is not purely external background. The model activities are taken from a different crystal (Belli), so the “measurement − model” difference also contains the disagreement between the intrinsic background of our crystal and the model.
  • Below ~238 keV the resolution is not confirmed — the shape of the model there is an extrapolation; a comparison with the measurement in this region is not interpreted.
  • Different instruments. The measurement is from the RC-103G (unit 1); the FWHM function of the model is based on the RC-103G (unit 2).
  • Near 352 keV the γ line of ²¹⁴Pb (351.9 keV) coincides in position with the α peak of the crystal; they cannot be separated in this region.
  • The energy scale of the measurement was checked at only one point. The SpectraVibe measurement tool accepted a single γ reference line in this file — ²¹²Pb 238.6 keV (the residual of the file scale is +2.1 keV against a tolerance of ±5.1 keV); the offset was checked at one point, and the slope and curvature of the scale were not verified, so the scale cannot be considered verified. Between July and December 2024, the α peak of the same instrument shifted by ~2 channels with identical calibration coefficients in the files — the scale near 350 keV is uncertain by approximately 5 keV.
  • Instrument name in the file. In the measurement file, the instrument is named “RadiaCode-110” (probably a software profile), while the instrument itself is an RC-103G (unit 1).
