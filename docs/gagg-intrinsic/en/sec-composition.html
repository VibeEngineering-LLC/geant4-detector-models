The impurity activities (¹⁷⁶Lu, ¹⁴⁷Sm, the U and Th chains) are taken from a different crystal — Belli, Table 1 (Epic-Crystal, 285.746 g); the basis for each row is given in the “Components” table; the exact composition of the RC-103G cannot be established from open data; according to Belli et al. (footnote 13), the radioactive contamination of GAGG:Ce crystals from different manufacturers differs strongly.

Where the uranium, thorium, and the rest in the composition come from. U, Th, Lu, and Sm are trace impurities: 41 mBq/kg of ²³⁸U corresponds to about 3·10⁻⁹ g of uranium per gram of crystal (≈ 3 ppb by mass), and 14 mBq/kg of ²³²Th to ≈ 3.5 ppb (our conversion using the specific activities of ²³⁸U and ²³²Th). They are not included in the mass fractions of the table (which reflect only the stoichiometry of the host material) and do not affect the radiation transport, but they are what produces the intrinsic background. Their activities are mostly not calculated but were measured by Belli et al. (in the model: ¹⁷⁶Lu — the upper limit from the RC-103G measurement, ¹⁴⁷Sm — the remainder of the Belli sum of 1555 minus the calculated ¹⁵²Gd, ²²⁸Ra taken equal to ²²⁸Th, ²³⁵U — an upper limit) on a 285.7 g GAGG:Ce crystal over 1336 h (Table 1): by fitting the α spectrum (the α events being separated by pulse shape; ¹⁵²Gd + ¹⁴⁷Sm, the U and Th chains), by time-amplitude selection of fast decay pairs, by delayed coincidences of ²¹²Bi–²¹²Po, and by fitting the β/γ spectrum (¹⁷⁶Lu; the pure α emitters ¹⁴⁷Sm and ¹⁵²Gd are not part of this fit). ¹⁵²Gd is not an impurity — it is a natural isotope of gadolinium (0.20%), and its activity is calculated from the composition.
