What was calculated and how

  1. Task. The model spectrum of the intrinsic radioactivity of a 10×10×10 mm GAGG cube and its decomposition into 12 components (“templates”); a comparison with the RC-103G spectrum measured in a lead shield. The external background (shielding, room, cosmic rays) is not modeled in this spectrum.
  2. Geometry and material. A bare cube in vacuum, without housing, SiPM, or shielding. The material is Gd₃Al₂Ga₃O₁₂, ρ = 6.63 g/cm³, without Ce (see the section “Initial composition of the crystal”).
  3. Source. For each template, the parent nucleus is at rest at a random point of the crystal volume (uniformly distributed) with an isotropic direction; Geant4 samples the decay and the entire sub-chain down to the specified terminating nuclide. Templates: T1 ¹⁵²Gd (α; terminated at ¹⁴⁸Sm — its T½ in the Geant4 RadioactiveDecay 6.1.2 data (z62.a148) is 2.209·10²³ s ≈ 7·10¹⁵ years, so it is unnecessary to follow the chain further); T2 ¹⁴⁷Sm; T3 ¹⁷⁶Lu (β⁻ to the 596.8 keV level of ¹⁷⁶Hf + the γ cascade of 306.6/201.8/88.3 keV — Geant4 data RadioactiveDecay 6.1.2 z71.a176 and PhotonEvaporation 6.1.2 z72.a176); T4 ²³²Th; T5 ²²⁸Ra→²²⁸Ac (terminated at ²²⁸Th); T6 ²²⁸Th→…→²⁰⁸Pb; T7 ²³⁸U→²³⁴Th→²³⁴ᵐPa (terminated at ²³⁴U); T8 ²³⁴U; T9 ²³⁰Th; T10 ²²⁶Ra→…→²¹⁴Po (terminated at ²¹⁰Pb); T11 ²³⁵U→²³¹Th (terminated at ²³¹Pa); T12 ²²⁷Ac→…→²⁰⁷Pb. Each template comprises 10⁶ parent decays (macro /run/beamOn 1000000); the random seeds are fixed and recorded in the file header. The templates are normalized “per parent decay,” so the activities can be changed without repeating the Monte Carlo calculation.
  4. Geant4 11.4.2 physics. The process list is G4EmStandardPhysics_option4 + G4RadioactiveDecayPhysics; atomic de-excitation comprises fluorescence and Auger emission with the production cut ignored, without PIXE (header line: cut_mm=0.0500 deex=deex fluo=1 auger=1 pixe=0 ignore_cut=1): characteristic X-rays (Gd K series ≈ 43 keV, Ga ≈ 9–10 keV, Gd L series ≈ 6–7 keV) are sampled in the model, and their escape from the cube is possible only near the surface; the escape is visible in the templates: for ²³²Th (T4), the 63.8 keV γ ray of the branch to the 64 keV level, absorbed in the Gd K shell, gives a Gd K-escape peak at 751–752 keVee (794 minus Gd Kα 42.3 and 43.0 keV; ≈ 84 counts out of 217 thousand events of the branch, 0.04% — from the histogram of the L72 template), and in the earlier templates of the external background for the same material (not used on this page) there is an escape peak at 32 keV (Pb X-ray 75 keV minus Gd Kα); the L series and the Ga X-ray were not singled out; PIXE (vacancies from α impact) is not included; a test with PIXE (templates T1, T2, T4, 10⁶ decays, three seeds, the same kB 0.0072) showed that each decay produces an additional ≈ 11 keV (≈ 50 keV for T4) of energy of secondary electrons and photons, which yield light 1:1, and the peak of the sum shifts upward by 9–29 keV; a diagnostic run on T1 (20 decays, tracking) showed the mechanism: these are ≈ 117 electrons per decay with energies up to 1 keV (90% below 0.1 keV), produced along the α step when PIXE is enabled (photons: 3 per 20 decays); that is, a part of the ionization loss of the α, which without PIXE stays within the α step and is quenched according to Birks, goes into unquenched electrons; this is apparently an accounting artifact (other explanations were not tested), not radiation physics, so the templates with PIXE were not recalculated and PIXE is not used for the α light yield; the production cut for secondary particles is 0.05 mm. The threshold for “very long-lived” decays is raised to 10⁷⁰ ns only for the ¹⁵²Gd template (line of the T1 macro: /process/had/rdm/thresholdForVeryLongDecayTime 1.0e+70 ns; it is compared with the mean lifetime, not with the half-life: τ(¹⁵²Gd) = 4.917·10³⁰ ns).
  5. Pulse pile-up. Energy deposits inside the crystal are summed into a single pulse if the decays occur within a 1 µs window (macro /gagg/window 1 us; assumption: the signal formation in the RC-103G is unknown); the window is applied to the local decay time (the time counted from the decay of the parent along the chain) within the decay tree, so short-lived daughters (²¹²Po, τ = 424.6 ns) fall into the same pulse, whereas long-lived ones (²¹⁴Po, τ = 235.8 µs) practically do not.
  6. Light yield (visible energy). e⁻ and γ: 1:1 with the energy deposition (the nonproportionality of GAGG is not modeled). α particles: the energy deposition is calculated step by step by Geant4, and the light yield follows Birks’ law at each α step: dL = S · dE/(1 + kB/ρ·(dE/dx)); (dE/dx) is the tabulated electronic stopping power from G4EmCalculator (ComputeElectronicDEDX) at the mean energy of the step; the α step is limited to 0.05 µm (/gagg/ionMaxStep 0.05 um). kB is a parameter of a particular crystal: for different types of crystals from the same manufacturer it differs by a factor of 1.7 (LANL, Table III); Geant4 has no kB for GAGG, so the value is taken from independent measurements with accelerator beams: Furuno et al., JINST 16 (2021) P10012, doi:10.1088/1748-0221/16/10/P10012, Table 2 — kB = 0.0065; Espinoza et al. (LANL), IEEE TNS 71 (2024) 2102, doi:10.1109/TNS.2024.3434380, Table III — 0.0072 (GAGG High Yield), 0.0093 (Standard), 0.0121 (Fast Decay), Advatech crystals; kB is in g·MeV⁻¹·cm⁻²; the stopping powers used by the authors: Furuno — SRIM, LANL — SRIM and ESTAR/PSTAR. kB is not fitted to the α particles of the intrinsic background or to our measurement. The scale S(kB) brings the α light yield to the γ scale: S = 662 / L, where L is the mean light yield of the full-absorption events of 662 keV γ rays calculated by Geant4 with the same kB (as in the normalization to the electron response in Tretyak’s method used at LANL); S = 1.025900 (kB 0.0065), 1.028459 (0.0072), 1.035977 (0.0093), 1.045701 (0.0121). Recoil nuclei: light yield 0 (an assumption; not calculated). T3 (¹⁷⁶Lu) has no α particles, and its template is unchanged. The main curve of the plot is kB = 0.0072 (LANL, GAGG High Yield; file model_sum_L72.json): kB was not fitted, but of the four literature values the one closest to the measurement was chosen, so +17.7 keV is the smallest of the four deviations, not a blind prediction and not a test of the model; the independent prediction is the entire band of the four kB values.

    Summary. (1) No literature kB reproduces the measured 349.8 keV: the closest, 0.0072, gives 367.5 keV for the maximum of the smeared ¹⁵²Gd+¹⁴⁷Sm sum (+17.7 keV; histogram step 1 keV, statistical uncertainty not calculated, the instrument scale near 350 keV is uncertain by approximately 5 keV); matching would require kB ≈ 0.0077 (an estimate by linear interpolation between 0.0072 and 0.0093, not a run). Because 0.0072 was chosen as the value closest to the measurement, +17.7 keV is not a test of the model; the independent prediction is the entire band of the four kB values. (2) In the range of the Belli formula (2.1–7.4 MeV), no kB agrees with the formula template simultaneously for ¹⁵²Gd and for ²¹⁵Po (at 0.0065: +12.09% and +2.02%; at 0.0072: +3.30% and −5.35%). α/γ increases from ¹⁵²Gd to ²¹²Po by a factor of 1.42–1.48 in the model versus 1.61 from the Belli formula — an extrapolation of the formula beyond its range; moreover, in Belli et al. the α scale above ~2 MeV on the γ scale is distorted by saturation of the digitizer. (3) The spread of the prediction (maximum of the Gd+Sm sum from 239.5 to 398.5 keV, ~159 keV) is mainly determined by the differences among the literature kB values — the crystal type and manufacturer, the measurement method (kB of different types from one manufacturer, Advatech, differs by a factor of 1.7); the contribution of the dE/dx calculation in Geant4 (G4EmCalculator instead of the SRIM and ESTAR/PSTAR used by the authors) has not been estimated. (4) The measured 349.8 keV peak is interpreted as the maximum of an unresolved ¹⁵²Gd+¹⁴⁷Sm mixture, on the grounds of a count rate close to the Gd+Sm sum of the model; therefore the maximum of the smeared sum, not the single ¹⁵²Gd line, is compared with it. Counterargument: the peak width of 31.5 keV is closer to that of a single line (model: mixture 43.0 keV, Gd alone 31.4 keV); the question is open, and the +17.7 keV comparison is tentative. The ²¹²Po line is almost invisible in the spectrum as a separate feature: 90.5% of its decays fall into one pulse with the β particle of ²¹²Bi (1 µs window).
    Fit (for comparison): variants A, B1, B2 The earlier version of the page showed variants A, B1, and B2 — values of kB fitted to the Belli formula (A — over the entire curve of 2.1–7.4 MeV; B1 — to the ¹⁵²Gd line) or to the measured peak (B2); therefore they are not suitable as a test of the model. ⚠ Variant B2 was fitted to ONE line, ¹⁵²Gd, at 349.8 keV, whereas 349.8 is the maximum of the ¹⁵²Gd+¹⁴⁷Sm mixture; the smeared sum of B2 is 357.7 keV, 7.9 keV above the measurement.
  7. Resolution. Gaussian smearing with the FWHM function = √(75.402 + 2.4752·E + 3.9295·10⁻⁴·E²) keV (the parameters are in model_sum_L72.json, field fwhm_params), fitted to the lines at 238.6–2614.5 keV of the RC-103G (unit 2); below 238 keV it is an extrapolation (shaded on the plot). The response tail is 0.
  8. Normalization. The spectrum of a component = template × activity (mBq/kg) × cube mass of 6.63 g. The activities are from Belli, Table 1, with the following departures: ²²⁸Ra is taken equal to ²²⁸Th (11.52 mBq/kg, an assumption of equilibrium; Belli et al. give only an upper limit, ≤ 4 mBq/kg, for ²²⁸Ra, and in the model it is 2.9 times as high), ²³⁵U (2 mBq/kg) is the upper limit of Belli et al.; ¹⁵²Gd is calculated (792.83 mBq/kg); ¹⁴⁷Sm is the remainder of the Belli sum of 1555 mBq/kg (762.17 mBq/kg); ¹⁷⁶Lu is the upper limit (4309 mBq/kg) from the RC-103G measurement; the rows of the “Components” table show the basis. The sum includes all components with weight 1. The conversion to counts uses the live time of 668,023.5 s.
  9. Measurement. The RC-103G spectrum (unit 1) in a lead shield, 22–30.12.2024, live time 668,023.5 s; the scale from the file was checked at one point — the ²¹²Pb 238.6 keV line (the only accepted γ reference line; the slope was not verified). In the file the instrument is named “RadiaCode-110” (probably a software profile), while the instrument itself is an RC-103G. Serial numbers and the file path are not shown on the page.
  10. What is not modeled. See the section “Limitations and caveats”.
