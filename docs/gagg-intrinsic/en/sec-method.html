What was calculated and how

  1. Task. The total model spectrum of the intrinsic radioactivity of a 10×10×10 mm GAGG cube and its decomposition into 12 components (response spectra); a comparison with the measured spectrum of the RC-103G in a lead shield. The external background (shielding, room, cosmic radiation) is not modeled in this spectrum.
  2. Geometry and material. A cube in vacuum, without housing, SiPM, or shielding. The material is Gd₃Al₂Ga₃O₁₂, ρ = 6.63 g/cm³, without Ce (see the section “Initial composition of the crystal”).
  3. Source. For each response spectrum, the parent nucleus is at rest at a random point of the crystal volume (uniformly distributed) with an isotropic direction; Geant4 samples the decay and the entire part of the decay chain up to the specified final nuclide, at which the calculation of the chain is terminated. Response spectra: T1 ¹⁵²Gd (α; final nuclide ¹⁴⁸Sm — its T½ in the Geant4 RadioactiveDecay 6.1.2 data (z62.a148) is 2.209·10²³ s ≈ 7·10¹⁵ years, so it is unnecessary to follow the chain further); T2 ¹⁴⁷Sm; T3 ¹⁷⁶Lu (β⁻ to the 596.8 keV level of ¹⁷⁶Hf + the γ cascade of 306.6/201.8/88.3 keV — Geant4 data RadioactiveDecay 6.1.2 z71.a176 and PhotonEvaporation 6.1.2 z72.a176); T4 ²³²Th; T5 ²²⁸Ra→²²⁸Ac (final nuclide ²²⁸Th); T6 ²²⁸Th→…→²⁰⁸Pb; T7 ²³⁸U→²³⁴Th→²³⁴ᵐPa (final nuclide ²³⁴U); T8 ²³⁴U; T9 ²³⁰Th; T10 ²²⁶Ra→…→²¹⁴Po (final nuclide ²¹⁰Pb); T11 ²³⁵U→²³¹Th (final nuclide ²³¹Pa); T12 ²²⁷Ac→…→²⁰⁷Pb. Each response spectrum comprises 10⁶ decays of the parent nuclide (macro /run/beamOn 1000000); the random number generator seeds are fixed and recorded in the file header. The response spectra are normalized “per decay of the parent nuclide,” so the specific activities can be changed without repeating the Monte Carlo calculation.
  4. Geant4 11.4.2 physics. The process list is G4EmStandardPhysics_option4 + G4RadioactiveDecayPhysics; atomic de-excitation — fluorescence and Auger electron emission with the production cut for secondary particles ignored, and with PIXE (the deex=max mode; file header line: cut_mm=0.0500 deex=max fluo=1 auger=1 pixe=1 ignore_cut=1): characteristic X-rays (Gd K series ≈ 43 keV, Ga ≈ 9–10 keV, Gd L series ≈ 6–7 keV) are sampled in the model, and their escape from the cube is possible only near the surface; the escape is visible in the response spectra: for ²³²Th (T4), the 63.8 keV γ ray of the branch to the 64 keV level, absorbed in the Gd K shell, gives a Gd K-escape peak at 751–752 keVee (794 minus Gd Kα 42.3 and 43.0 keV; ≈ 84 counts out of 218 thousand events of the branch, 0.04% — from the histogram of the earlier response spectrum without PIXE; in the response spectrum with PIXE, channels 748–753 contain 755, 685, 812, 330, 132 and 45 counts on the right-hand slope of the α line, where the escape peak is not resolved), and in the earlier response spectra of the external background for the same material (not used on this page) there is an escape peak at 32 keV (Pb X-rays 75 keV minus Gd Kα); the L series and the Ga X-rays were not singled out; PIXE (X-ray emission on filling of vacancies created by impact ionization by an α particle) is included; its effect was tested separately (response spectra T1, T2, T4, 10⁶ decays, three random number generator seeds, the same kB 0.0072): without special treatment, each decay produces an additional ≈ 11 keV (≈ 50 keV for T4) of energy of secondary electrons and photons, which give a scintillation response of 1:1, and the channel of the histogram maximum shifts upward by 9–29 keV (the centroid in the window around the line, by 9–19 keV); a diagnostic run on T1 (20 decays, track tracing) showed the mechanism: these are ≈ 117 electrons per decay with energies up to 1 keV (90% below 0.1 keV), produced along the α transport step when PIXE is enabled (photons: 3 per 20 decays); that is, a part of the ionization losses of the α, which without PIXE stays within the α transport step and is subject to quenching according to Birks’ law, goes into electrons whose scintillation response is not quenched; the process that creates these electrons is ionization by the α (ionIoni). Therefore in the application such electrons are counted as part of the α track (scintillation response as for the α, option /gagg/lowEQuenchKeV 2; in the file header, lowE_quench_keV=2), and the sum with PIXE returns to the case without PIXE: the centroid in the window around the line is, for T1, 358.97 (without PIXE), 368.08 (PIXE, electrons give a scintillation response of 1:1), 359.22 (PIXE, electrons as part of the α track); for T2, 377.48 / 387.40 / 377.53; for T4 (window 774 ± 30 keV), 756.19 / 775.14 / 756.90 keVee (10⁶ decays, three random number generator seeds, kB 0.0072). For the β/γ response spectra T3 (¹⁷⁶Lu) and T5 (²²⁸Ra→²²⁸Ac), PIXE does not change the scintillation response: the difference of the histograms is within the statistics (χ²/n 1.00–1.07), and the shift of the mean scintillation response is ≤ 0.6 keV with a random sign. Therefore, when the scintillation response is accounted for correctly, PIXE affects the response spectra by not more than 0.7 keV. All 12 response spectra on which the page is built were calculated in the PIXE + quenching mode (10⁶ decays, random number generator seeds of the earlier response spectra; the response spectrum T12 was obtained from 20 segments of 5·10⁴ decays each at all kB, because at kB 0.0072 a calculation in a single run terminated abnormally; at kB 0.0065, 0.0093 and 0.0121, T6 (25 segments of 4·10⁴), T7 (5 segments of 2·10⁵) and T10 (20 segments of 5·10⁴) were also assembled from segments). Compared with the earlier response spectra without PIXE, at kB 0.0072 the total model spectrum differs by −0.0003%, the maximum of the Gd+Sm sum remained 367.5 keV, in none of the 868 channels with a sum count rate above 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹·keV⁻¹ did the sum change by more than 1.3%; the positions of 46 of 47 α lines shifted on average by −0.34 keV (by not more than 1 keV — one channel; 25 did not change), and for one weak component of the ²²⁷Th doublet the position is ambiguous according to the search algorithm. At the other kB (0.0065, 0.0093, 0.0121) the maximum of the Gd+Sm sum also did not change (398.5, 298.5 and 239.5 keV), and of the 40 values in the table of lines below, 22 changed (over all four kB), all downward by 0.09–1.00 keV (on average by 0.65 keV; not more than one channel). The production cut for secondary particles is 0.05 mm. The threshold for “very long-lived” decays is raised to 10⁷⁰ ns only for the ¹⁵²Gd response spectrum (line of the T1 macro: /process/had/rdm/thresholdForVeryLongDecayTime 1.0e+70 ns; it is compared with the mean lifetime, not with the half-life: τ(¹⁵²Gd) = 4.917·10³⁰ ns).
  5. Pulse pile-up of successive decays of one chain. Energy depositions inside the crystal are summed into a single pulse if the decays occur within a 1 µs window (macro /gagg/window 1 us; assumption: the signal formation in the RC-103G is unknown); the window is applied to the decay time counted from the decay of the parent nuclide along the chain, within the decay chain (including branchings), so short-lived daughter nuclides (²¹²Po, τ = 424.6 ns) fall into the same pulse, whereas long-lived ones (²¹⁴Po, 235.8 µs) practically do not.
  6. Scintillation response (in electron-equivalent energy). For e⁻ and γ, the response is proportional to the energy deposition in the ratio 1:1 (the nonproportionality of the scintillation response of GAGG is not modeled). α particles: the energy deposition is calculated by Geant4 in steps of the trajectory calculation (particle transport steps), and the scintillation response follows Birks’ law at each α step: dL = S · dE/(1 + kB/ρ·(dE/dx)); (dE/dx) is the tabulated ionization (electronic) stopping power from G4EmCalculator (ComputeElectronicDEDX) at the mean energy of the step; at each step the ionization energy losses and the scintillation response they create are computed, and the transport step of the α trajectory calculation is limited to 0.05 µm (/gagg/ionMaxStep 0.05 um) so that Birks’ law is applied to locally almost constant energy losses per unit path length. kB is a parameter of a particular crystal: for different types of crystals from the same manufacturer it differs by a factor of 1.7 (LANL, Table III); Geant4 has no kB for GAGG, so the value is taken from independent measurements with accelerator beams: Furuno et al., JINST 16 (2021) P10012, doi:10.1088/1748-0221/16/10/P10012, Table 2 — kB = 0.0065; Espinoza et al. (LANL), IEEE Trans. Nucl. Sci. 71 (2024) 2102, doi:10.1109/TNS.2024.3434380, Table III — 0.0072 (GAGG High Yield), 0.0093 (Standard), 0.0121 (Fast Decay), Advatech crystals; kB is in g·MeV⁻¹·cm⁻²; the stopping powers used by the authors: Furuno — SRIM, LANL — SRIM and ESTAR/PSTAR. kB is not fitted to the α particles of the intrinsic background or to our measured spectrum. The normalization factor S(kB) brings the scintillation response of the α to the γ energy scale (the electron equivalent): S = 662 / L, where L is the mean scintillation response of the full-absorption events of 662 keV γ rays calculated by Geant4 with the same kB (as in the normalization to the electron response in Tretyak’s method used at LANL); S = 1.025900 (kB 0.0065), 1.028459 (0.0072), 1.035977 (0.0093), 1.045701 (0.0121). Recoil nuclei: scintillation response 0 (an assumption; not calculated). T3 (¹⁷⁶Lu) has no α particles, and its response spectrum is unchanged. The main curve of the plot is kB = 0.0072 (LANL, GAGG High Yield; file model_sum_L72q.json): kB was not fitted, but of the four literature values the one closest to the measured spectrum was chosen, so +17.7 keV is the smallest of the four deviations, not a blind prediction and not a test of the model; the independent prediction is the entire band of the four kB values.

    Summary. (1) No literature kB reproduces the measured 349.8 keV: the closest, 0.0072, gives 367.5 keV for the maximum of the ¹⁵²Gd+¹⁴⁷Sm sum with broadening (+17.7 keV; histogram channel width 1 keV, statistical uncertainty not calculated, the uncertainty of the energy calibration of the instrument near 350 keV is approximately 5 keV); agreement would require kB ≈ 0.0077 (an estimate by linear interpolation between 0.0072 and 0.0093, not a calculation result). Because 0.0072 was chosen as the value closest to the measured spectrum, +17.7 keV is not a test of the model; the independent prediction is the entire band of the four kB values. (2) In the range of the Belli formula (2.1–7.4 MeV), no kB agrees with the calculation by the Belli formula simultaneously for ¹⁵²Gd and for ²¹⁵Po (at 0.0065: +12.09% and +1.99%; at 0.0072: +3.28% and −5.41%). α/γ increases from ¹⁵²Gd to ²¹²Po by a factor of 1.42–1.47 in the model versus 1.61 from the Belli formula — an extrapolation of the formula beyond its range; moreover, in Belli et al. the energy calibration of the α above ~2 MeV in units of γ energy is distorted by saturation of the digitizer (ADC). (3) The spread of the prediction (maximum of the Gd+Sm sum from 239.5 to 398.5 keV, ~159 keV) is mainly determined by the differences among the literature kB values — the crystal type and manufacturer, the measurement method (kB of different types from one manufacturer, Advatech, differs by a factor of 1.7); the contribution of the dE/dx calculation in Geant4 (G4EmCalculator instead of the SRIM and ESTAR/PSTAR used by the authors) has not been estimated. (4) The measured 349.8 keV peak is interpreted as the maximum of an unresolved ¹⁵²Gd+¹⁴⁷Sm mixture, on the grounds of a count rate close to the Gd+Sm sum of the model; therefore the maximum of the sum with broadening, not the single ¹⁵²Gd line, is compared with it. Counterargument: the peak width of 31.5 keV is closer to that of a single line (model: mixture 43.0 keV, Gd alone 31.4 keV); the question is open, and the +17.7 keV comparison is tentative. The ²¹²Po line is almost invisible in the spectrum as a separate feature: 90.5% of its decays fall into one pulse with the β particle of ²¹²Bi (1 µs window).
    Fit (for comparison): variants A, B1, B2 The earlier version of the page showed variants A, B1, and B2 — values of kB fitted to the Belli formula (A — over the entire curve of 2.1–7.4 MeV; B1 — to the ¹⁵²Gd line) or to the measured peak (B2); therefore they are not suitable as a test of the model. ⚠ Variant B2 was fitted to ONE line, ¹⁵²Gd, at 349.8 keV, whereas 349.8 is the maximum of the ¹⁵²Gd+¹⁴⁷Sm mixture; the sum of B2 with broadening is 357.7 keV, 7.9 keV above the measured value.
  7. Resolution. Gaussian broadening of the lines with the energy dependence of the FWHM = √(75.402 + 2.4752·E + 3.9295·10⁻⁴·E²) keV (the parameters are in model_sum_L72q.json, field fwhm_params), fitted to the lines at 238.6–2614.5 keV of the RC-103G (unit 2); below 238 keV it is an extrapolation (shaded on the plot). The tail of the response function is taken as 0.
  8. Normalization. The spectrum of a component = response spectrum × specific activity (mBq/kg) × cube mass of 6.63 g. The specific activities are from Belli, Table 1, with the following departures: ²²⁸Ra is taken equal to ²²⁸Th (11.52 mBq/kg, an assumption of equilibrium; Belli et al. give only an upper limit, ≤ 4 mBq/kg, for ²²⁸Ra, and in the model it is 2.9 times as high), ²³⁵U (2 mBq/kg) is the upper limit of Belli et al.; ¹⁵²Gd is calculated (792.83 mBq/kg); ¹⁴⁷Sm is the difference up to the Belli sum of 1555 mBq/kg (762.17 mBq/kg); ¹⁷⁶Lu is the upper limit (4309 mBq/kg) from the measured spectrum of the RC-103G; the rows of the “Components” table show the justification. The sum includes all components with weighting factor 1. The conversion to counts uses the live time of 668,023.5 s.
  9. Measured spectrum. The RC-103G spectrum (unit 1) in a lead shield, 22–30.12.2024, live time 668,023.5 s; the energy calibration from the file was checked at one point — the ²¹²Pb 238.6 keV line (the only accepted reference γ line; the slope was not verified). In the file the instrument is named “RadiaCode-110” (probably a software profile), while the instrument itself is an RC-103G. Serial numbers and the file path are not shown on the page.
  10. What is not modeled. See the section “Limitations and caveats”.
